## The problem with the market

Walk into any hiring conversation about AI agents in 2026, and you will hear the same words: *LangChain*, *RAG*, *vector databases*, *prompt engineering*.

None of these are wrong. All of them are surface-level.

The real question is: **can this person build a system that survives production?**

Because there is a massive gap between a demo agent that works on a Tuesday afternoon and a production agent that handles 10,000 requests a day without hallucinating, without leaking data, and without costing you a fortune in API bills.

## What actually separates a good AI engineer

### 1. They think in systems, not prompts

Anyone can write a prompt. A senior AI engineer designs the entire pipeline: input validation, retrieval, reasoning, output guardrails, evaluation, monitoring, fallback behavior.

### 2. They have shipped something real

Ask for one production system. Not a notebook. Not a demo. A system that real users interact with. If they cannot point to one, they are still learning.

### 3. They understand the cost curve

LLM APIs are cheap until they are not. A good AI engineer knows how to design for cost — caching, batching, model tiering, and when to reach for a smaller model instead of the biggest one.

### 4. They measure everything

You cannot improve what you do not measure. A production AI system needs evaluation metrics, trace logs, and failure analysis. If your candidate has never heard of agent evaluation, that is a red flag.

## The questions to actually ask

Instead of "Have you used LangChain?", ask:

- *Walk me through a system you built that failed in production. What broke, and how did you fix it?*
- *How do you evaluate whether your agent is performing well?*
- *What is your approach to prompt injection and data leakage?*
- *When would you NOT use an LLM?*

The answers will tell you more than any certification.

## What this means for you

If you are hiring an AI engineer in 2026, stop asking about tools. Start asking about systems.

The engineer who can design, build, and operate a production AI system is rare. The engineer who can list 20 frameworks is everywhere.

Choose accordingly.
